## How Much Does Demolition Cost in Georgia?

The single number homeowners ask for is the total. In Georgia, full structural demolition is priced per square foot because crew size, equipment, and haul distance scale with the building.

| Project type | Typical cost in GA | Notes |
|--------------|-------------------|-------|
| Small home (≤1,000 sq ft) | $4,000–$10,000 | Shed/garage/cottage |
| Standard home (1,500–2,500 sq ft) | $8,000–$25,000 | Most common |
| Large home (3,000+ sq ft) | $20,000–$45,000 | More equipment, longer haul |
| Garage / outbuilding | $1,500–$6,000 | Often separate permit |
| Commercial / industrial | $2–$8 per sq ft | Asbestos survey usually required |

These ranges reflect Georgia conditions: red-clay excavation, metro Atlanta traffic that slows haul trucks, and county tipping fees that vary from ~$35 to ~$70 per ton depending on the landfill.

### What Drives the Price Up
- **Asbestos or lead paint**, a required survey adds $400–$900, and abatement (if found) can add several thousand dollars. Older Georgia homes, especially pre-1980, are the risk group.
- **Foundation removal**, leaving a slab is cheaper; full basement or crawl-space removal adds $3,000–$10,000.
- **Access**, tight Atlanta-suburb lots, wooded rural parcels, or proximity to wetlands (Army Corps jurisdiction) raise equipment and labor time.
- **Haul distance**, the farther the debris travels to a Georgia landfill or recycling yard, the higher the per-ton cost.

## Do You Need a Permit to Demolish in Georgia?

**Yes, in every county.** Georgia has 159 counties, and each county's building department issues its own demolition permit. There's no scenario where you can legally demolish a permanent structure without one. The permit exists so the county confirms utilities are disconnected, asbestos is cleared, and the work won't endanger neighbors.

### What Counties Typically Require
Most Georgia county permit offices ask for:
- A completed demolition permit application
- Proof of **utility disconnection** (gas, electric, water, sewer)
- An **asbestos survey** letter (statewide expectation for built structures)
- A site plan showing property lines and adjacent structures
- Sometimes a **refuse/disposal plan** showing where debris goes

Fulton, Gwinnett, Cobb, and DeKalb (metro Atlanta) often move fastest online. Rural counties like those in South Georgia may still run permits through the courthouse, adding a few days. Either way, plan for **3–15 business days** of permit review before any wrecking begins.

> **Real example:** A Kennesaw homeowner wanted a 1,800 sq ft ranch torn down. The county required utility disconnection letters and an asbestos survey up front. Once submitted, the permit cleared in 6 business days. Demolition took 2 days; debris hauling another day. Total timeline: 9 days from application to cleared lot.

## How Long Does Demolition Take?

From "we're ready" to "lot is clear," expect **1 to 4 weeks**.

1. **Permitting (3–15 days)**, the variable step. Submit complete paperwork and it's quick; missing the asbestos letter and it stalls.
2. **Site prep (1–2 days)**, utility cut, fencing, rodent/utility locates.
3. **Demolition (1–5 days)**, a single home is usually down in a day; large or commercial structures take longer.
4. **Debris hauling & clearing (1–4 days)**, load-out, grading, final sweep.

Weather matters. Georgia's spring thunderstorms and occasional winter ice can add a day or two, but the state's mild climate keeps demolition viable year-round.

## What Happens to the Debris?

A common fear is that everything goes to a landfill. In practice, a lot is recycled:
- **Concrete** is crushed and reused as road base or fill, many Georgia yards accept it directly.
- **Clean wood** is chipped or sent to biomass/paper mills.
- **Metals** (rebar, fixtures) are pulled for scrap value.
- **Drywall/insulation** and mixed debris go to the county landfill at the per-ton tipping fee.

Recycling concrete on-site or at a nearby yard can shave meaningful dollars off the haul bill, which is why crews that crush and reuse tend to come in lower on total cost.

## Residential vs. Commercial Demolition in Georgia

Residential teardowns are smaller, permit through the county building department, and rarely need an environmental Phase I. Commercial and industrial demolition in Georgia almost always requires an **asbestos survey by a licensed Georgia firm**, utility coordination, and sometimes Georgia EPD notification for larger footprints. The cost per square foot is often lower on big buildings, but the absolute number is far higher.

## Cost by Georgia Region (Why Location Changes the Number)

Georgia isn't uniform, and demolition pricing follows the terrain.

- **Metro Atlanta (Fulton, Gwinnett, Cobb, DeKalb, Forsyth):** Highest haul and permit efficiency, but traffic slows trucks and landfill tipping fees run toward the top of the state range. Expect the upper half of the $4–$15/sq ft band for most homes.
- **Coastal (Chatham, Bryan, Liberty, Glynn):** Salt air, sandy soils, and Army Corps wetlands jurisdiction add planning steps. Proximity to Savannah's port means some debris can be recycled cheaply, but wetland buffers can delay permits.
- **Central (Bibb/Macon, Houston/Warner Robins, Muscogee/Columbus):** Mid-range costs, straightforward county permits, good recycling yard access.
- **South Georgia (Albany, Valdosta, Waycross):** Lower tipping fees and shorter hauls often land projects in the lower cost band, though rural access can add equipment mobilization time.
- **North Georgia mountains (Dahlonega, Blue Ridge):** Sloped lots and rock excavation can push costs up; septic and well disconnection add steps a city parcel wouldn't need.

## How to Keep Your Demolition Budget Under Control

You can't change your county's tipping fee, but you can control the rest:

1. **Get the asbestos survey done early.** Discovering asbestos after the crew arrives is the most common budget killer. Order the survey before you get quotes.
2. **Decide on the slab up front.** Keeping a foundation lowers cost now but may complicate your rebuild. Know your next-step plan before demolition starts.
3. **Ask about recycling.** A crew that crushes concrete on-site or at a nearby yard usually beats one that hauls everything to the landfill.
4. **Bundle selective work.** If you only need a garage, deck, or chimney gone, say so, partial demolition costs a fraction of a full teardown.
5. **Get a written estimate after a site visit.** Phone-quote-only contractors hide the variables that become your surprise fees.

## Your Pre-Demolition Checklist

Walk this list before you call a crew, and your permit sails through:

- [ ] **Confirm ownership & lien status**, counties won't permit a teardown on a property you don't clearly own or that has open liens.
- [ ] **Order the asbestos survey**, required for most built structures; get the letter before quoting.
- [ ] **Disconnect utilities**, gas, electric, water, and sewer must be cut and confirmed in writing.
- [ ] **Pull the county permit**, submit the application with the survey and disconnection letters.
- [ ] **Mark underground lines**, Georgia 811 locates before any digging.
- [ ] **Confirm debris destination**, know which landfill or recycling yard your crew uses and the per-ton fee.
- [ ] **Get the written estimate**, signed, itemized, with permit and haul included.

## County Permit Offices: What to Expect

Every Georgia county runs its own building department, but the rhythm is similar:

- **Metro counties (Fulton, Gwinnett, Cobb, DeKalb):** Online portals, 3–7 day turnaround, e-payments common.
- **Mid-size (Bibb, Muscogee, Houston, Richmond):** Mix of online and in-person; 5–10 days typical.
- **Rural South Georgia:** Often in-person at the courthouse or planning office; 7–15 days, so pad your schedule.

If your project sits near a wetland, stream, or the coast, the Army Corps of Engineers may also need to sign off, that's the one variable that can add weeks, not days. We handle permitting support across all 159 counties so you're not navigating 159 different offices alone.
